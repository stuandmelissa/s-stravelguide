# Safety and Altitude

Safety guidance should be calm, specific, and conservative.

## Altitude
- Ascend gradually when possible.
- Hydrate normally; avoid overhydration.
- Eat regular meals.
- Keep the first high-elevation morning moderate.
- Headache, nausea, unusual fatigue, dizziness, or poor coordination can indicate altitude illness.
- Descend and seek medical help for severe or worsening symptoms, confusion, trouble walking, chest tightness, or shortness of breath at rest.

## Heat
- Start desert parks early.
- Carry more water than expected.
- Avoid exposed midday hikes.
- Treat headache, nausea, weakness, or confusion as warning signs.

## Wildlife
- Never approach or feed wildlife.
- Stay inside the vehicle when animals are close to the road.
- Bison and moose require substantial distance.
- Bear spray should be accessible, not buried in luggage.

## Driving
- Switch drivers before fatigue becomes obvious.
- Avoid pushing through severe weather, low visibility, or wildlife-heavy darkness.
- Fuel near half a tank in remote areas.

The app is a travel aid, not a substitute for official park alerts, emergency services, or medical care.
